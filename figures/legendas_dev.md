# Figure legends (dev)

**Fig. 1 | São Paulo read as a tissue.** **a**, Correspondence between spatial transcriptomics and the city: parcels are cells, census tracts are spots, streets and stations are the interstitium and vessels, neighbourhood composition is the niche, crime generators and targets play the role of ligand–receptor pairs and crime is the phenotype. **b**, 1.65 million fiscal parcels coloured by type (Leiden on 30 native parcel attributes from the 2025 property-tax cadastre and the December-2025 business registry, linked by address), with census favelas (IBGE FCU) as pseudo-cells; rail lines and water bodies as landmarks. **c,d**, Parcel-resolution views of the centre (**c**) and of a periphery where a favela abuts high-rise condominiums (**d**), with census-tract outlines (white). No crime data are shown. Scale bars, 10 km (**b**) and 500 m (**c,d**).

**Fig. 2 | Parcel types and tissue architecture.** **a**, UMAP of 150,000 parcels coloured by type. **b**, Marker attributes per type (dot size, fraction of parcels expressing the attribute; colour, mean standardized value). **c**, Spot (census-tract) composition as pie charts in the centre, analogous to spot deconvolution. **d**, Neighbourhood enrichment between parcel types (z-scores against 100 permutations restricted to district × population-density decile; diagonal omitted). **e**, Tissue domains: tract composition smoothed over contiguous neighbours and clustered (k = 7, chosen by silhouette), named by their most enriched elements. **f**, Typing stability across subsamples (adjusted Rand index) against the number of types; the selected resolution (orange; 16 types, ARI = 0.950) was fixed without any crime data; dashed line, agreement with an OAC-style k-means baseline (ARI = 0.40).

**Fig. 3 | Crime is a phenotype of the cell classes of the tissue.** **a**, Phone thefts and robberies attributed to each cell class in 2025 (per km of parcel frontage or street; diamonds, per station), from a Poisson model in which each tract's count is the sum of what its cells produce (EM; 95% intervals from 500 district bootstrap resamples): high-rise residential and mixed parcels 37 km⁻¹ yr⁻¹, mixed residential and commercial parcels 13, arterial streets 16, rail stations 73 per station; low-rise residential parcels are near zero. **b**, Signature matrix of 15 crime phenotypes (0.69 million reconciled events in exploration tracts, 2024–2025): dot size, share of the phenotype's expected count produced by the class; colour, log₂ enrichment against the mean phenotype. The most enriched class differs across phenotypes (10 distinct classes); traffic injuries, a positive control, have their highest rate per km among street classes on arterial streets (4.46 vs 0.23 injuries per km on local streets over 2024–2025). **c**, Point validation per phenotype: Spearman correlation between the attributed rate and the rate observed by linking geocoded events to the nearest street segment and its abutting parcels (median across parcel types 0.78); phenotypes with less than 80% geocoded events are not tested. **d**, Crime programs from non-negative matrix factorization of the tract × phenotype matrix (k = 4, the smallest k ≥ 3 stable across seeds, stability 0.96); rows named by the cell classes most associated with each program. **e**, Program usage per tract (rasterized; spatial-vault districts in grey). **f**, Decomposition of expected 2025 phone thefts by theoretical covariate group and cell class (exact Shapley values against the average tract; reductions as a sink). All crime analyses exclude 19 districts held out as a spatial vault.

**Fig. 4 | Each class keeps its own daily rhythm.** **a**, Pre-specified test of the class clock (claim C4), phone theft first and phone robbery second: departures of each class from the city's share of events in four periods of the day, 2024 against 2025 (dots, class × period; phone theft r = 0.96, phone robbery r = 0.94), and the class contrast H (×100) against three nulls: events permuted between periods within districts (grey dots, 200 permutations) and blocks of events from the same tract and period on the same day or in the same month permuted together (bars, median to 95th percentile; 999 permutations). P values below the axis (phone theft: P ≤ 0.005 with events, P ≤ 0.001 with day blocks and P ≤ 0.001 with month blocks in 2024 and P ≤ 0.005 with events, P ≤ 0.001 with day blocks and P ≤ 0.001 with month blocks in 2025; phone robbery: P ≤ 0.005 with events, P ≤ 0.001 with day blocks and P = 0.002 with month blocks and P ≤ 0.005 with events, P ≤ 0.001 with day blocks and P ≤ 0.001 with month blocks). **b**, Exploratory: hourly profile of phone robbery per cell class estimated by the attribution model (2024–2025, events with exact hour). **c**, Weekday robbery (line, model estimate) and pedestrians on the street (grey; Origin–Destination survey 2023) in four classes (low-standard houses, favelas, houses with shops (manufacturing), rail stations). **d**, Citywide robbery risk per pedestrian by hour relative to the daily mean (weekdays; shading, 95% interval from 1,000 household resamples of the survey); between 00:00 and 05:59 it is 4.6 times the daily mean (4.2–5.1). **e**, Weekend-to-weekday ratio of daily rates of phone theft and phone robbery per class (bars, 95% intervals from 200 district bootstrap resamples; robbery on low-standard houses, 0.05, 95% interval 0.00–0.34). **f**, Robbery per km against robbery per pedestrian across classes (Spearman ρ = 0.64, 95% interval 0.51–0.69).

**Fig. 5 | The tissue is stable, places remember, and rates move.** **a**, Out-of-sample R² of the 2025 tract theft rate (district-blocked cross-validation): parcel types 0.22, the same attributes without typing 0.26, tissue 0.27, tissue plus tract covariates 0.32, historical mean 0.48; between-tract share of variance (ICC) = 0.84. **b**, Between-tract variance of the log annual theft rate (2022–2025): tissue (expected count from composition alone) 42%, stable memory beyond the tissue (covariance of residuals across years) 46% and transient 23%; in high-crime tracts (≥10 expected per year) memory reaches 60%. **c**, Persistence of the residual beyond the tissue: r = 0.69, 0.64 and 0.63 at lags of 1–3 years. **d**, Annual transition rates between parcel families in the property-tax cadastre, 2012–2025 (2021 excluded); permanence = 97.5% per year. **e**, Phone thefts fell by 20% from 2022 to 2025 with the tissue held at its 2025 composition, but not uniformly: local streets fell faster than the city (relative ratio 0.76, 95% interval 0.62–0.96), while rail stations kept their rate (72 → 73 per station) and arterial streets fell less than the city (relative ratio 1.18, 1.02–1.41); dot size, share of 2022 thefts. **f**, Change 2022→2025 by phenotype (phone robbery -40%, phone theft -2%, assault +50%).

**Fig. 6 | Month-to-month variation is concentrated and poorly forecast.** **a**, Share of 2025 phone crime captured when flagging the highest-risk tracts: the ensemble captures 28% in 1% of the city and 53% in 5%, against 52% for the historical mean, 52% expected if the ensemble's forecasts were exact and 65% for an ex-post oracle (all 96 districts). **b**, R² reached by the ensemble (filled) and noise ceiling (open) for between-and-within and within-unit variation, by spatial and temporal scale (exploration districts); for tract × month, 0.80 of 0.92 between and within, 0.018 of 0.56 within tracts. **c**, Cumulative shares of phone crime and of extra-Poisson monthly variance across tracts ranked by count; the 1% of tracts with most crime hold 34% of phone crime and 88% of the extra-Poisson variance. **d**, Difference in continuous ranked probability score (CRPS, against real-valued reconciled counts) between each method and the negative binomial GLM (dots; bars, 95% district bootstrap intervals; filled, Holm-adjusted P < 0.05 against the best baseline); the ensemble improves CRPS by 0.016 (6%). **e**, Share of flagged area (top 5%) and of recorded phone crime by tract income quintile, with the ratio of predicted to recorded counts.
