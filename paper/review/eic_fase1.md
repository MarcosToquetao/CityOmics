D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by me). This asks whether the manuscript is organised so that a busy reader can follow one storyline, whether the exposition is clear and economical, whether figures and legends are of publication quality and agree with the text, and whether the manuscript follows the venue's Article conventions: a title of at most 90 characters, an unreferenced abstract of at most 150 words, a main text of at most about 3,500 words structured as an untitled introduction followed by Results and Discussion, Methods of at most about 3,000 words, roughly 50 references in Nature numbered superscript style, and a figure budget appropriate to an Article.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by me). This asks whether the manuscript belongs in Nature Cities and whether it makes an original and significant contribution for that readership: urban scientists, planners and policymakers who are not specialists in either criminology or spatial omics. It covers whether the central idea is new relative to the closest prior work, whether the methodological borrowing yields insight that existing approaches could not, whether the significance reaches beyond one city or one data system, and whether the claims are framed in proportion to the stage of evidence (here, a development-stage analysis with confirmation pending).

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: Compliance with Nature Cities Article limits (title at most 90 characters, abstract at most 150 words without references, main text at most 3,500 words as untitled introduction plus Results plus Discussion, Methods at most 3,000 words, about 50 references in Nature superscript style, figure count proportionate); a single thesis statable after the abstract and first paragraph; every Results subsection, figure and panel serving that thesis; identical numbers, sample sizes and terminology across abstract, Results, Discussion, Methods and legends; figures that show what the text says they show; borrowed jargon defined at first use; economical sentences without hedging stacks or repetition.
what_triggers_block: The manuscript could not go to external review as submitted because several hard format limits are breached at once, or the narrative splits into multiple loosely connected stories so that no single thesis can be stated, or figures and legends contradict the text on headline results.
what_triggers_warn: Format is broadly compliant and one storyline is identifiable, but there are passages, numbers or figure panels that do not serve the thesis, small inconsistencies in numbers or terms between sections and legends, undefined borrowed vocabulary, recurrent wordy or unclear sentences, or deviations from Nature reference style.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: Whether the question matters to a broad Nature Cities readership; whether the core idea is new relative to the closest environmental-criminology and urban-form literature and is positioned against it explicitly; whether the spatial-transcriptomics borrowing delivers an urban-scientific insight rather than a relabelling; whether significance extends beyond the study city and data source; whether planning or policy implications are proportionate; and whether development-stage results are framed as exploratory, with headline claims conditioned on the pending confirmation.
what_triggers_block: The contribution is within scope but, as framed, it is not yet appropriate for the venue because headline claims are stated as established or general while resting on development-stage exploration, or novelty against the closest prior work is not demonstrated, or the borrowed analogy carries the argument without yielding a distinct urban insight; repairable through reframing, explicit positioning or a sharper statement of what is new.
what_triggers_warn: Fit and contribution are credible, but significance for non-specialist readers is under-articulated, positioning against the closest prior work is thin, generalisation beyond the study city or policy relevance is asserted rather than argued, or the analogy is sometimes decorative rather than explanatory.
what_triggers_fatal: The work falls outside the scope of Nature Cities, or its core contribution reduces to known findings renamed in borrowed vocabulary, so that no revision within the Article format could produce a contribution appropriate to the venue.
