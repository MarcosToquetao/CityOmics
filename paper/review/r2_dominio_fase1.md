## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat). This dimension asks whether the design, the data handling, the statistical reporting and the reproducibility affordances would clear a referee's bar in the field. From a domain seat, I note only where a design choice has a domain consequence (for example, how police-recorded events are cleaned, deduplicated, geocoded and aggregated to spatial units, or how temporal windows are defined); I do not score it, because the dimension is not eligible for my role.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by my seat). This is the dimension I score. It asks whether the paper's claims agree with current evidence in environmental criminology and crime-and-place research, whether prior work is represented faithfully (each citation supports the sentence it is attached to, and original sources are credited), and whether domain terminology and the domain reading of results are free of factual error. For a paper on recorded crime in a large Latin American city that borrows its analytic vocabulary from spatial transcriptomics, this covers: correct use of routine activity theory, crime pattern theory, crime generators and attractors, guardianship and place management, ambient population and the law of crime concentration; an accurate account of what is and is not new relative to micro-place, trajectory, typology and built-environment studies of crime; a sound reading of temporal and offence-specific patterns (robbery, mobile-phone theft); explicit handling of the reporting and recording biases of police data in the Brazilian context; and prevention implications that the evidence can carry.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate, co-eligible for methodology). It asks whether the central thesis is internally consistent and whether the evidence supports the claims without fallacies. From a domain view, a coherence failure often surfaces as a theoretical label that the analysis does not actually test; I will report such cases under D2 only when the defect is one of domain accuracy, and otherwise leave the dimension unscored.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high priority, owned by the perspective seat). It asks whether framing, definitions and implications are accessible and substantiated for adjacent-field readers. The biological analogy is the obvious cross-disciplinary surface; I will comment only where an analogy causes a criminological concept to be misdescribed, and I do not score this dimension.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal priority, owned by the journal-fit seat). It asks about organisation, clarity, figures and tables, and adherence to venue conventions, including reference style. I may list copyedit-level items about criminological terms as minor issues, but I do not score this dimension.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the journal-fit seat). It asks whether the manuscript fits the venue and makes an original, significant contribution for its readership. My D2 assessment of whether the stated contribution over existing crime-and-place research is accurate will be relevant input, but the venue-fit judgement and its score belong to the journal-fit seat.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether every citation supports the specific sentence it is attached to and credits original sources; whether routine activity theory, crime pattern theory, crime generators/attractors, guardianship/place management, ambient population and the law of crime concentration are defined and applied as the field defines them; whether the novelty claimed over micro-place concentration, longitudinal trajectory, place-typology, street-network and built-environment studies of crime is stated accurately; whether the domain reading of temporal patterns, offence-specific results (robbery, mobile-phone theft), spatial heterogeneity and change over time is consistent with established evidence or is argued as a departure from it; whether reporting, recording, classification and geocoding biases of Brazilian police records are acknowledged where an interpretation depends on them; whether the key literature a criminology referee would expect is present; and whether prevention implications are proportionate to descriptive or correlational evidence.
what_triggers_block: A core claim (headline finding, stated mechanism, or stated novelty) misrepresents or contradicts established crime-and-place evidence without acknowledging it; or a citation that carries a core claim does not report what the sentence attributes to it; or a criminological theory is misdefined in a way that drives the interpretation of a core result; or a core interpretation ignores a reporting or recording bias of police data that could plausibly produce the same pattern; or prevention prescriptions are stated as warranted when the evidence is only descriptive; each of these is repairable by rewriting, re-citation, added literature or added caveats.
what_triggers_warn: Citation mismatches confined to peripheral or background sentences; missing literature that the audience expects but whose absence does not change a core claim; imprecise or inconsistent criminological terminology; novelty phrasing that is overstated in tone but whose substantive contribution survives; or a domain interpretation that is plausible but under-argued or under-referenced.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis or headline empirical finding rests on a domain error that cannot be repaired by rewriting, such as a pattern that the paper's own description of the data shows to be an artefact of how events are recorded or located, or a claimed contribution already fully established in prior crime-and-place work so that nothing substantive remains, or core claims supported by references that do not exist.
