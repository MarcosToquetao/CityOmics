### Calibration Status

`NOT_CALIBRATED`

### D3 score rationale

My Phase 1 plan holds; there is no dissent. D3 is scored block (repairable). The decisive defect is C1. The paper's own estimates contradict its headline claim as worded: composition is said to "fix" a place's crime rate, yet tissue is the minority component of the stable between-tract variation, and the tract's own history out-predicts it about two to one. The block is not fatal. The design can discriminate between the rivals I raise: geocoded-only reanalysis, region-specific class rates, reporting-robust phenotypes and the pending vaults. A narrower thesis (composition structures which crime and when, and part of how much) is supported by the evidence shown. Eleven MAJOR findings add to the case for block. Several of them (M2, M3, M8) are rival explanations that the text names or implies but never bounds.

### What the paper does well

The design safeguards are unusually strong for urban crime research. A spatial vault and a temporal vault back a pre-specified Holm family, and the parcel typing was fixed without crime data, which removes an obvious circularity. The paper reports where its analogy ties or loses, which is rare. It gives a minimum detectable effect for its null event study and includes a positive control (traffic injuries on arterials). Splitting the predictability ceiling into between-unit and within-unit parts is a reusable idea. These strengths make the paper worth arguing with. They do not repair the problems below.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

The paper's own numbers describe a city where recorded crime is chiefly a property of where it was before, and only partly of what a place contains. Out of sample, tissue predicts 2025 tract rates with R² = 0.25. The tract's own history reaches 0.48. In the variance decomposition, the stable residual beyond tissue (48%, and 58% in high-crime tracts) exceeds the tissue share (42%). Robbery falls by 40% while composition stays fixed. The one quasi-experimental test of the tissue–crime link, business openings and closures, is null. The attribution is a non-negative Poisson regression of recorded counts on land-use exposures. Its identifying assumption is one rate per class everywhere, which is exactly what the memory result rejects. The "signatures" may therefore describe where classes sit. The point validation reuses events that also build the reconciled counts. That reconciliation, which allocates ungeocoded events by the preceding twelve months, mechanically adds persistence and damps monthly variance. "Month-to-month crime is noise" is a failure to reject at about half an event per tract-month, where the test has almost no power. Within tracts, forecasts reach 3% of their own ceiling, and robbery fell in exactly the fabric declared noise. The dawn clock is credited to guardianship that was never measured, using night-time denominators from a 2017 weekday survey that are close to zero. Robbery of commuters on their way to transit, assigned to housing frontage because bus stops are not a class, is at least as plausible. A simpler summary: recorded crime in São Paulo persists in place and is partly structured by land use. The phenotype vocabulary adds rhetoric, not inference, as the Discussion concedes.

### Direct answers to the panel's weighting questions

1. **Phenotype, or relabelled land-use correlates?** Partly a relabelling. Two components are genuine methodological additions at this scale: the additive exposure model, a composition-aware inversion of the areal unit, and the joint signature matrix over 15 phenotypes. The phenotype claim proper says crime is expressed by the tissue. It is not established, for three reasons. Tissue is the minority share of stable variance (C1). The only causal test is null (M7). The constant-rate assumption is contradicted by the memory result (M1). The Discussion itself withdraws the descriptive reading (M9).
2. **Does memory undercut the tissue thesis?** Yes, in three ways. In magnitude, it is larger than tissue (C1). In identification, it violates the assumption that makes class rates interpretable (M1). In narrative, the Discussion ends by redirecting research effort to memory. Memory is also partly exposed to a data-preparation artefact (M2). That cuts both ways: an artefact would shrink memory, but it would also shrink the "noise" result.
3. **Is "month-to-month crime is noise" a power or aggregation artefact, and does it contradict the decline?** It is largely a power artefact (M3), possibly helped by reconciliation (M2). It is also an aggregation slip: "close to the limit" holds for between-unit prediction only. The decline does not contradict it logically, because tract-month noise can coexist with a slow aggregate trend. It does contradict the interpretation. The systematic, class-differentiated temporal change sits in the diffuse fabric the paper calls noise (M4).
4. **Do the alternative explanations threaten the conclusions?** Reconciliation and geocoding suppression threaten the memory and noise findings directly (M2). Reporting bias graded by income threatens the signatures and clocks, because the classes themselves are income-graded (M8). Mis-measured exposure (2017 weekday survey, no bus-stop class) threatens the guardianship reading of the clock (M6). Police deployment and citywide interventions threaten the reading of the decline (M4). None of these is bounded in the text.
5. **Is there one coherent story, and is anything superfluous?** No. There are three stories (M9): a substantive thesis about São Paulo, an audit of whether omics tools transfer, and a cross-domain forecasting benchmark. The title states the first. The final sentence states a different "central contrast". Several elements serve none of the stories directly (minor items m4–m7).

### Story-line audit (title to final sentence)

- Title and Discussion ¶1 claim that crime is a phenotype of tissue and that composition fixes it. This is overstated relative to Results §Stable tissue (C1).
- Introduction: the stated gap (reading composition, crime type and rhythm together) fits the attribution and clock sections. The ecological-fallacy motivation is undercut by the model's own ecological assumption (M1).
- Results §A city read as a tissue: typing and favela pseudo-cells are needed. Neighbourhood enrichment and tissue domains are not used downstream (m4).
- Results §Each cell class: attribution and signatures are on-story. NMF programs are not used again (m4).
- Results §Clock: on-story, but the guardianship reading is unsupported (M6).
- Results §Stable tissue: this section turns the story from tissue to memory and decline (C1, M4). The event study is the strongest test of the thesis, but it is presented as an aside (M7).
- Results §Month-to-month: the forecasting benchmark and its cross-domain framing introduce a second borrowing narrative (m6). The language-model decision layer is off-story (m5). The pseudo-vault paragraph reads like confirmatory evidence (m7).

- Final sentence: the "central contrast" is geography against monthly noise. That is not the title's claim, and its noise half is the weakest-supported result (M3).

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **Persistent place effects as the primary structure.** Crime begets crime where it already is: offenders learn their awareness spaces, places acquire reputations, near-repeat dynamics accumulate. This is more parsimonious than "phenotype of tissue" and fits the finding that history beats tissue out of sample. The paper reports the residual but does not test any account of it.
2. **Recording and reporting processes graded by income and place.** Reporting varies with online access, insurance, the need for a police report to block a phone, trust in police and territorial control. These map directly onto income-graded classes and could produce part of the class signatures and clocks.
3. **Data-preparation artefacts.** Ungeocoded events are allocated in proportion to lagged geocoded counts, and coordinate suppression at homes and stations is non-random. Residence-stratum and station-stratum allocation therefore rests on a selected subset of events, which matters for the residential-burglary signature the Discussion uses for prevention.
4. **Exposure mis-measurement.** Frontage understates activity on high-rise parcels, where units are stacked vertically. Favela exposure is synthetic (households × 7.0 m). The crowd comes from a 2017, weekday, pre-pandemic survey. Bus stops, where dawn commuter robbery plausibly happens, are not a class, so their crime is absorbed by housing and local-street classes.
5. **Policing and citywide interventions.** Hubs have dedicated policing and convenient reporting points, so stable hub rates could reflect saturation policing or reporting convenience rather than a "niche". A robbery-specific fall in 2022–2025 could reflect phone-blocking and anti-theft measures, camera programmes introduced around 2023–2024 (e.g., the federal Celular Seguro scheme and the municipal Smart Sampa programme, if applicable to the study window), police operations, or reclassification between robbery and theft.
6. **Offender-side routines and journey-to-crime.** Dawn robbery in the periphery may reflect offenders' proximity to home and their own schedules, not missing guardians.
7. **Territorial governance by organized crime in the periphery.** This could shape both the geography of lethal violence and the stable residual ("memory"), and it is a social structure rather than tissue.
8. **Methodological paths not taken.** Risk-terrain modelling, or segment-level negative binomial land-use regression with segment fixed effects, as the urban head-to-head for attribution. Spatially varying class rates. Hierarchical partial pooling to estimate month-to-month signal in the diffuse fabric. An explicit power analysis for the dispersion test.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- Residents of favelas and low-income peripheries, whose classes are labelled as producing lethal violence and cargo robbery (risk of territorial stigma at class level).
- Victims who do not report (the dark figure), and the victimization-survey perspective.
- The recording institutions (civil and military police, the online reporting channel), whose practices generate the data.
- Commuters and transport operators (bus, rail, metro), who are at the locus of dawn robbery.
- Communities exposed to predictive deployment, given the income distribution of flagged areas.
- Territorial power held by organized crime, which is not a stakeholder to consult but is a power structure missing from the account.

### Unexamined Premise

The cell analogy assumes that a crime belongs to the place where it is recorded, and that the parcel is the unit that expresses the phenotype. In tissue, the cell expresses the gene. In the city, neither the offender, nor the victim, nor the recording officer is part of the tissue. They move through it. The premise drives three things: the productive language ("what its cells produce"), the identification of crime with a property of parcels, and the treatment of everything that is not a parcel (people, routines, institutions) as a residual "memory". None of the analogy's tests in Extended Data Figs. 2–5 examines this premise. A paper that kept the instrument but dropped the premise would be more defensible.

### Observations (Non-Defects)

- The two vaults and the pre-specified five-claim Holm family are a real strength. The deliberate confirmation placeholders are appropriate at this stage. My concern is only how the pseudo-vault is framed (m7).
- Fixing the parcel typing without crime data avoids an obvious circularity in the attribution.
- The equivalence audit, which reports ties and losses for the analogy, is commendable. It is also the best evidence for the "instrument" reading of the paper over the "phenotype" reading.
- Reporting a minimum detectable effect for the null event study turns a null into an informative bound.
- The traffic-injury positive control is a useful sanity check of the exposure model.
- Splitting the predictability ceiling into between-unit and within-unit parts may be the paper's most transferable idea once reframed as in M3.
- The fairness check of forecast flags by income quintile is good practice.
