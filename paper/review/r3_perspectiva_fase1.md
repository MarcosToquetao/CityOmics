## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor). The design, data handling, statistics and reproducibility must clear the ordinary peer-review bar of the field. This belongs to the methodology seat. From my cross-disciplinary seat it matters only indirectly: a transfer of omics methods can only be judged "real" if the borrowed estimators are run in a form that a methodologist would accept, so I will lean on, not duplicate, that seat's judgement.

D2 (domain_accuracy). Claims must agree with current evidence in the home domain (urban science, criminology, spatial statistics), prior work must be represented correctly, and domain terms and results must be stated without factual error. This belongs to the domain seat. My only overlap is the terminology of the source discipline: when a spatial-omics term is borrowed, its source meaning must be correct, which I treat under D4 rather than D2.

D3 (argumentative_coherence). The core thesis must hold together internally, the evidence must support the claims, and no fallacy may undermine the central argument. This belongs to the devil's advocate (and methodology). I will not audit internal consistency or logic; I note only that an analogy that changes meaning between sections is a coherence risk that the DA, not I, scores.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance). This is my dimension. A reader from an adjacent field (here, an urban scientist or policy reader encountering spatial-omics vocabulary, and a computational biologist encountering urban data) must be able to follow the framing, definitions and implications, and every interdisciplinary claim must be backed. For an analogy-driven transfer that means three things: each imported concept must be technically faithful to its source method or have its departure stated; the analogy must work as an instrument that generates tests or falsifiable consequences rather than as decoration; and the gain over existing urban and criminological tools must be shown and stated with honesty. It also covers the practical, policy and ethical reach of the work for the communities whose places are being typed and predicted.

D5 (writing_and_structure). Organisation, clarity, figure and table quality and venue conventions belong to the journal-fit seat. I will flag clarity problems only where they block an adjacent-field reader from understanding an imported concept, and I will route them to D4.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution). Fit to the venue and the originality and significance of the contribution for its readers belong to the journal-fit seat. My input is limited to whether the cross-disciplinary novelty, if real, is the kind of contribution an urban-science readership would value; I do not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: whether each imported spatial-omics concept (cell, spot, phenotype, deconvolution, signature matrix, expression programme, niche or neighbourhood, clock, noise) is technically faithful to the source method or its departure is stated; whether each analogy is used as an instrument with a stated test or falsifiable consequence rather than as metaphor; whether the omics vocabulary is defined at first use for an urban readership and kept to what the analysis needs; whether the claimed gain over established urban-science, criminology and spatial-statistics methods is demonstrated and stated honestly; and whether the practical, policy and ethical consequences of producing area-level crime phenotypes (predictive policing, recorded-crime bias, equity, stigmatisation of low-income areas and informal settlements) are addressed.
what_triggers_block: the method transfer is the main claimed contribution yet one or more load-bearing analogies are technically misdescribed relative to the source method and no analysis shows a gain over conventional urban or criminological methods; or area-level crime predictions or risk typologies are offered for use without any treatment of predictive-policing feedback, recorded-crime bias or stigmatisation of the communities mapped.
what_triggers_warn: analogies are broadly faithful but at least one is overstated, loose or decorative without a test; omics vocabulary is used without definitions an urban reader can follow, or more terms are imported than the analysis needs; the cross-disciplinary gain is asserted more strongly than shown, or adjacent urban-science and criminology precedents are not credited; or ethical and policy implications are acknowledged only generically rather than tied to the specific outputs.
