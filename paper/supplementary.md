## Supplementary Methods

### Reproducibility of the parcel typing

The typing reported in the article was computed on the current attribute table (1,613,440 parcels with a cadastral record) with an exact k-nearest-neighbour graph, so that labels do not depend on the number of threads; rerunning the full procedure reproduced it (ARI = 1.00). Across eight subsamples of 80,000 parcels, the raw clusters agreed with ARI = 0.95 (minimum 0.91) when the resolution was chosen and 0.97 in a later rerun, the 16 final types with ARI = 0.98 (minimum 0.96), and each subsample agreed with the final typing with ARI = 0.97–0.99. An earlier typing (12 types) had been computed on a version of the attribute table before about 35,000 repeated parcel polygons were unified. It did not reproduce on the current table (ARI = 0.58 with the current types): its two street-retail types fell almost entirely into the current mixed residential and commercial type, and its low-rise residential types split by whether the parcel hosts a registered business. The earlier typing, the atlas built on it and the corresponding version of the manuscript are preserved in the repository.

### Tissue architecture and turnover

Neighbourhood enrichment used a k-nearest-neighbour graph (k = 8) and 100 permutations restricted to district by population-density decile (Squidpy; Palla et al., Nat. Methods 2022); tissue domains came from smoothing tract composition over contiguous neighbours and k-means (k = 7, chosen by silhouette). Annual transitions between parcel families came from a gradient-boosting classifier of family from cadastral attributes (87.8% accuracy on held-out parcels), applied to the cadastres of 2012–2025; pairs involving 2021 were excluded because cadastral coverage drops by about 25,000 parcels that year.

### Event study of business openings and closures

For the event study, openings and closures of bars and restaurants and openings of retail establishments were dated from the registry and linked to street segments, in two variants: any establishment, and only establishments other than individual micro-entrepreneurs on parcels with commercial or service use. The outcome was street crime (phone theft and robbery and other street theft and robbery) per segment-month. A segment was treated at its first such event in 2023–2024 with none in the preceding 12 months; later events were kept (16–29% of treated segments had another within 11 months). For each treated segment and event month e, the change in geocoded street crime from its mean in months −12 to −7 was compared with the same change in the mean of never-treated segments (no such event in 2022–2025) of the same district and street class, and averaged over treated segments (pre-trend, e = −6 to −1; effect, e = 0 to 11), a difference-in-differences with never-treated controls (cf. Callaway & Sant'Anna, J. Econom. 2021). Intervals come from 200 bootstrap resamples of districts of treated segments, with control means fixed; the reported range is the union of the 95% intervals relative to baseline, and 80% power corresponds to effects of 5–7% (9% for the strict variants).

### Exploratory analogy audit

Extended Data Figs. 6–9 compare each omics-derived module with an urban analogue on the same data: neighbourhood enrichment against the colocation quotient, criminological ligand–receptor scores (generator and target layers combined within 250–1,000 m and tested by permutation) against bivariate Lee's L, MISTy views against a spatial lag, interaction terms against additive models and near-repeat excitation by the Knox ratio (400 m, 1–14 days). A module wins when it adds out-of-sample skill or a quantity its analogue cannot produce, ties when both give the same reading and loses when the analogue is simpler and no worse.

**Lethal violence, district × quarter** (96 districts, fitted on 2022Q1–2024Q4, evaluated on 2025Q1–Q4; the same models are run for phone crime at the same scale): historical mean of the previous 4 quarters; Poisson GLM on log(1 + x) of the previous quarter and the 4- and 8-quarter means, with log population as a regressor (L2 10⁻⁶); empirical-Bayes rate shrunk towards the mean of contiguous districts (prior weight of 2 quarters); discrete Hawkes μ~d~ + α Σ~k≤4~ e^−β(k−1)^ y~t−k~ by maximum likelihood; LightGBM (Poisson, 7 leaves, min. 20 rows, rate 0.03, L2 5, 300 rounds) on lags, quarter, log population and four district-level theory scores. NB2 with global φ per model by maximum likelihood on the fitting window; Brier event y ≥ 1.

**Scores, as implemented.** For each tract-month the predictive distribution is NB2 with mean μ and dispersion φ (variance μ + μ²/φ). φ is one global value per model, fitted by maximum likelihood on the rounded observations of the fitting window, using the model's in-sample means (the ensemble uses its validation window). The CRPS is integrated exactly against the real-valued reconciled count, which is the linear interpolation between the CRPS of the adjacent integers (an earlier implementation scored a fractional count as the next integer; corrected before any 2026 data were opened); the log score uses the rounded count; the Brier score is for P(Y ≥ 1) against 1{y ≥ 0.5}; the share of crime captured ranks tracts by predicted mean per km² within each month, up to 1% or 5% of the city's area. Paired differences against the GLM use 2,000 bootstrap resamples of the 96 districts with Holm correction across the 12 comparisons.

**Temporal availability of inputs.** Each feature for month m was built from information available at the start of m. Crime history counted only reports registered before m. Monthly covariates were shifted by their publication lag (one month for property transactions and business registrations, three for service requests to the city, two for night lights, none for weather and the calendar). The 2022 census and the 2022 vulnerability index enter as static tract attributes, and annual population was interpolated between the 2010 and 2022 censuses and updated with the property-tax cadastre of the same year, which is issued in January. Business stocks were reconstructed month by month from the opening and final-status dates in the September 2026 snapshot of the business registry, which records only the final status and current address. Infrastructure layers, street lighting and OpenStreetMap features are 2026 snapshots without history. These static or final-status inputs make the benchmark retrospective rather than strictly real-time. The parcel typology of the atlas was built after the benchmark and was not used by any forecasting model.
